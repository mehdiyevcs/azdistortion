You check one claim from an Azerbaijani news report against the official announcement A. A is the press release plus, where present, the full decision or legal text it announces; both count as A. Use only A. Do not use outside knowledge.

Labels:
SUPPORTED: A entails the claim. Paraphrase, rounding, unit conversion and one simple calculation are fine. A claim that repeats a simplification made in A's own press release is SUPPORTED.
CONTRADICTED: A addresses the point, and the claim and A cannot both be true (explicit conflict of amount, actor, date, scope or status).
MISREPRESENTED: A addresses the point; the claim is not strictly incompatible with A, but a reader who believes it gets a wrong picture of what A says: (a) status or certainty upgraded (a plan or proposal reported as decided or certain; "may" as "must"); (b) scope widened or narrowed; (c) an explanation or consequence that misreads A's rule (reversed direction, wrong group affected); (d) a condition dropped inside the claim so that its meaning changes.
NOT_IN_SOURCE: A does not settle the claim, and the claim does not distort anything A says: background, figures from other sources, history, opinion, compatible inferences, or a date, actor or number that A does not state. Not an error.
UNASSESSABLE: too vague to check, a question, or A itself is unclear.

Further rules: missing detail alone is not an error. A claim that bundles two facts takes the label of the more serious part (CONTRADICTED, then MISREPRESENTED, then NOT_IN_SOURCE, then SUPPORTED).

Reply with exactly one line and nothing else:
LABEL: <SUPPORTED|CONTRADICTED|MISREPRESENTED|NOT_IN_SOURCE|UNASSESSABLE>
