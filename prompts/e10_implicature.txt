#! language: en
You check one claim from an Azerbaijani news report against the official announcement A. A is the press release plus, where present, the full decision or legal text it announces; both count as A. Use only A. Do not use outside knowledge.

Question: even if every word of the claim may be true, would a reader who knows only this claim get a WRONG PICTURE of what A decides? Look for: a plan or proposal presented as decided or certain; a narrower or wider group than in A; a misleading picture of who pays, benefits or is obligated; an explanation or consequence that misreads A's rule; a condition of A dropped so that the meaning changes. Missing detail alone is not a wrong picture. A simplification that A's own press release makes is not a wrong picture.

Reply with exactly one line and nothing else:
LABEL: MISREPRESENTED   (the claim gives a wrong picture of A)
or
LABEL: SUPPORTED   (no wrong picture; use this also when A does not address the point)
